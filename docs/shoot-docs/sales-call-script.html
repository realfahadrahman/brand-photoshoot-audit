The Sales Call

One call, two halves: probe first, present second. Cole's rule from 2026-08-19 stands: figure out what they actually want before quoting anything. The probe is not small talk before the pitch, it is where the sale happens. The structure is Hormozi's CLOSER with Brunson's 4-Question Close providing the digging questions, and trial closes sprinkled throughout (small yes-questions every minute or so: "make sense?", "right?", "fair?").

Never quote a price in DMs. The reply to any price question, including "how much for 25 edited pics", is:

"Depends what the shots are for. Different goals need totally different shoots. You free for a 15 minute call this week? I'll tell you exactly what I'd do in your case, and if it's not a fit I'll tell you that too."

Frame (first 60 seconds)

Set the agenda so the call is a consultation, not a pitch. Consultants get answered honestly; salesmen get haggled with.

"Here's how I run this. I'll ask you a few questions about what you're going for, then I'll tell you exactly what I'd do if I were you, and what it costs. If it's not a fit, I'll say so and point you somewhere cheaper. Sound fair?"

That "sound fair" is the first micro-yes.

C — Clarify why they're here

Get them to say the reason out loud. Their words become the pitch.

  • "What made you reach out now, as opposed to six months ago?"
  • "Where are these photos going to live? Dating profile, IG, LinkedIn, all of it?"
  • "What are you using right now, and what's wrong with it?"

L — Label the problem

Repeat it back, sharpened, and get agreement.

"So if I'm hearing you right: you look good in person, but your online presence doesn't match, and it's costing you [matches / credibility / clients]. Is that fair?"

O — Overview past attempts (the pain dig)

Brunson's question 2: why don't you have it yet?

  • "Have you tried fixing this before? Friend with a camera, selfies, an old shoot?"
  • "How'd that go?"
  • "Why do you think it didn't work?"

Let them conclude the old way failed. Do not say it for them. The usual answers, which the offer is built to counter: nobody directed them, they didn't know what to wear, the photos were random rather than a system, they felt awkward.

S — Sell the vacation

Sell the after-state, not the shoot. Thirty seconds, their words from step C plugged in.

"Here's what this looks like. Before anything, we figure out your archetype: who you actually are online, so the fits and locations and poses all say one thing instead of ten. You get a prep protocol so you show up at your physical peak. Shoot day, everything's decided in advance: you show up with a bag we told you to pack, we direct every shot, one afternoon. You walk away with up to nine finished shots, every single one postable, and a 30 day map of exactly what to post where. A month from now your online presence finally matches [the thing they said in C]."

Trial close: "Can you see why that beats 25 random edited pics?"

E — Explain away concerns

Take objections in the order they usually come.

"How much?" Present the one package the probe pointed to. Stack first, price last:

"For what you described, it's The Main Character package. Archetype session, prep protocol, shot map, three full looks across up to three locations, finished edits on every final, the 30 day posting map, and we rebuild your profile with the new shots. Separately that's about C$2,600 of work. It's C$1,500."

Then stop talking. Silence after price. First one to speak loses the frame.

"That's a lot for photos."

"For photos it would be. Photographers charge C$300 and hand you a folder, and you're back where you started with prettier raw material. You're not buying photos, you're buying the system that decides what to shoot and what to do with it after. That's the part you told me was missing."

"My friend has a good camera."

"The camera was never the problem. Did your friend plan your looks, direct your poses, and sequence a month of posts? That's the product. The camera's maybe 10 percent of it."

"I just need a few pics."

Downsell to a smaller package, never a smaller price: "Then The Profile Fix is your play. Two looks, C$1,000. Same system, smaller dose."

"Only 9 photos for $1,500?"

"Nine postable photos. You told me what the folder of 40 near-duplicates got you. Every final we hand you is a different version of you, edited, sequenced, ready to go. It's not a photo count, it's a month of presence."

"Let me think about it."

"Totally fine. What specifically do you want to think over: whether it'll work, or the money? Because those are different conversations." Then handle the real one. If it is genuinely timing, book the follow-up on the call, never "text me whenever".

R — Reinforce the decision

The sale is not done at yes. Buyer's remorse starts in minutes.

  • Take the deposit on the call (50 percent to book the date). A yes without money is a no with better manners.
  • Immediately send the archetype questionnaire and prep protocol. They should be doing homework within the hour; motion kills remorse.
  • "You're going to get the prep checklist tonight. Most guys say the prep alone was worth it. Talk soon."

Disqualify honestly

Tell people it is not a fit when it is not: budget under the smallest package, or wanting one headshot for a resume. Point them somewhere cheap and ask who they know. A clean no generates referrals; a squeezed bad-fit client generates refund demands and bad word of mouth in a small city.

Scoreboard per call

Log every call: source, package quoted, objection hit, outcome (closed / follow-up booked / disqualified / lost). Five numbers weekly: calls booked, calls held, closes, cash collected, average package. Volume before optimization; do not rewrite this script until it has run ten times.

The likely self-sabotage

Quoting price in the DM to avoid the call, and discounting on the call to avoid the silence after the price. Both are the same flinch. The script exists so the flinch has nowhere to go.
